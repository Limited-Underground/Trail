# OT-061 — First physical OpenTrail Heltec boot

Date: 2026-08-16
Status: PASS — experimental target evidence; not supported hardware

## Scope

One isolated `OT-DEV-001` Heltec V4 bench unit was selected as the
sacrificial-first target. `OT-DEV-002` remained disconnected and untouched.
No emulator, simulator, LoRa peer, GNSS, display, or GPIO-sensitive path
participated. After the verified boot, one physical Android phone performed an
exact-service-filtered discovery check only.

The owner explicitly accepted destruction of the unit's existing MeshCore
firmware and private configuration, required no private flash backup, and
authorized exactly one full-chip erase plus one write of the frozen OpenTrail
image set. Any later MeshCore recovery remains owner-operated through the
official MeshCore web flasher.

## ROM entry/exit rehearsal

Before the destructive operation, the isolated unit entered ESP32-S3 ROM mode
through the physical `PRG`/`RST` sequence. Pinned esptool 5.3.1, at 115200 baud
with no RAM stub, one open attempt, and one connection attempt, established only
these allowlisted facts:

- ESP32-S3 revision v0.2
- 40 MHz crystal
- 16 MiB external flash
- Secure Boot disabled
- flash encryption disabled

Raw tool output, the private serial port, MAC, and device identifiers stayed in
process memory and were not retained. No flash bytes or eFuse contents were
read. With `PRG` released, a manual `RST` returned the same public `Heltec V4
OLED` / MeshCore `v1.16.0-07a3ca9` runtime with an empty queue and zero device
or receive errors. A USB-only disconnect did not remove battery power and was
not treated as a recovery failure.

The ESP-IDF v6.0.2 image settings were DIO bootstrap, 16 MiB, and 80 MHz.
Esptool configuration forced one open attempt, one connection attempt, and one
write-block attempt. The single `write-flash --erase-all` invocation completed
successfully. A standalone `verify-flash` invocation then compared only the
four newly written public regions and exited zero. The board remained in ROM
until verification succeeded; no failed image was booted and no retry occurred.

## First boot result

After verification, the owner pressed `RST` once with `PRG` released. Windows
reported the ESP32-S3 USB interfaces present and healthy. A privacy-safe,
read-only twelve-second serial observation found at least two of the target's
five-second `heartbeat elapsed_ms=` records and found no boot-self-check failure,
runtime failure, panic, abort, or failed assertion.

Reaching the heartbeat loop proves that the deterministic boot self-check gate
and the target's NimBLE runtime start call returned successfully on this unit.
It does not by itself prove external BLE visibility, pairing, application
authorization, Ready state, command exchange, protected persistence, or a
second device.

## Physical BLE discovery

The exact accepted Android debug APK was rebuilt byte-for-byte: 9,677,165 bytes
with SHA-256
`9CE206EEEAE2B13FC5C1092CEF41C226607FD3A9905A5797D4EBE31F3DC7F01C`.
Its validation remained 135 JVM tests across thirteen suites with zero
failures, errors, or skips, plus warning-as-error lint reporting no issues. The
APK was installed without automatically granting permissions on one physical
Android 13/API 33 phone.

After the owner selected Bluetooth mode and explicitly allowed the compatible
device scan, the app reported exactly one nearby compatible device. The owner
did not select, connect, or pair with that candidate. No device name, address,
identifier, screenshot, or unrelated scan result was retained. This is bounded
evidence that the flashed target's privacy-safe OpenTrail service advertisement
was physically visible to the Android client; it is not GATT, authorization,
Ready, command, delivery, range, coexistence, or support evidence.

## Boundaries

- The OLED remaining blank is expected; this image does not drive it.
- LoRa, GNSS, display, and GPIO-sensitive behavior were not enabled or tested.
- Protected authorization storage remains deliberately denied.
- Exact received minor board revision, RF variant, physical QIO/PSRAM clocking,
  antenna, pinout, power/endurance, and regulatory status remain unresolved.
- This unit is `experimented`, not compatible, validated, supported, or
  production-ready.
- No additional erase, write, recovery, or unit-2 authority remains.
- The historical phone-independent evidence calculation advances from 30% to
  31% because this closes its prior first-target physical flash/runtime gate.
  Current V1 Companion and V2 Integrated release scoring remains unmeasured
  until its replacement model is explicitly approved.
